Model & Defence

Double Logistic (Bi-Sigmoid)
h(t) = c + a1 / (1 + exp(-k1 (t - t01))) + a2 / (1 + exp(-k2 (t - t02)))

The stage rises in one rapid pulse of about 7 m and then only partly recedes to a lower, flatter stage rather than settling at its peak, a shape a single logistic or Gompertz cannot reproduce because both are monotonic. A second, negative-amplitude logistic stacked on the first reproduces exactly that: one sigmoid for the inflow surge and an opposing sigmoid for the drawdown that follows it, each easing into a flat segment.

Residual Diagnostics

The residual mean is 0.00000 m (essentially zero, no overall drift), but the scatter is not free of structure: sign changes only 17 times across 288 points -- far fewer than the roughly 144 runs pure noise would produce -- so the errors come in short streaks rather than flipping randomly, the signature of a shape mismatch rather than logger noise. The spread nearly triples during the surge window (std 0.1136 m) compared with the quiet pre-event segment (std 0.0908 m) and the settled post-event segment (std 0.0356 m), and the single largest residual is 0.2208 m at Jul-22 06:15 -- 22x the logger's own 1 cm resolution. The fit is good (R²=0.9991) but not perfect: the true rise is sharper than a smooth double logistic can trace, exactly where the model earns its worst marks.

Quad integration of the fitted curve gives 1260.9708 m·hr (quad-reported absolute error 5.09e-07 m·hr). The trapezoid rule on the raw readings gives 1260.9963 m·hr, a gap of -0.0254 m·hr (-0.0020%) -- small because the fit tracks the data closely (R²=0.9991); the gap is exactly the area under the residual curve, positive where the fit overshoots the readings and negative where it undershoots. This number is a depth integrated over time in meter-hours, not a volume -- turning it into a volume would need the reservoir's stage-area curve, which this log does not contain.
